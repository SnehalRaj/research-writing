# Check the scientific claim

## Theorems

Write the objects, spaces, conventions and quantifiers before polishing a statement.
Trace each proof step to an assumption or an established result.
Check dimensions, zero inputs, boundary cases and composition order when relevant.
Distinguish local statements from global ones and existence from a construction.
Check that summaries and captions preserve the theorem's conditions.

Inspect each strict inequality and identify the assumption that makes it strict.
Check whether a local condition supports the global conclusion being claimed.
Keep necessary rank, regularity and nonzero conditions in the theorem itself.

A finite numerical check can expose a counterexample.
It cannot prove an unrestricted theorem.
Keep the failing input and numerical tolerance in the working record.
A source transcription and a validity check answer different questions.
If the source itself appears wrong, document the conflict for scientific review.

## Data and experimental results

Identify the observation unit, population and selection procedure.
Check units, denominators, metric definitions and the baseline protocol.
Distinguish a percentage-point difference from a relative percentage change.
Retain the number of seeds, folds or samples that supports the summary.
State how repeated measurements depend on one another.

Trace the input through preprocessing, model, objective, prediction and metric.
Keep training, validation and test decisions separate.
Check whether baselines share data, tuning rules and resource budgets.
Keep simulation, sampled noise and hardware execution distinct.

Recompute reported summaries from supplied data when possible.
Report missing inputs directly in working notes.
Keep uncertainty procedures faithful to the available observations.
Avoid an invented significance test, confidence interval or performance measurement.
A seed average on a fixed test set alone does not quantify uncertainty across tasks or datasets.

Preserve a meaningful improvement with its actual scale and conditions.
An insignificant or mixed result can still answer a research question.
Explain that answer directly.

## Literature and citations

Verify a new citation against its primary source and intended version.
Confirm that its actual theorem or experiment supports the sentence.
Record a page, equation or result locator when useful.
Check identifiers and rendered bibliography links after a citation edit.
A passing build alone supplies no source verification.

Keep access models, resource definitions and comparison tasks explicit in advantage claims.
A larger state space alone establishes no learning advantage.
Use an author's original source when a secondary summary leaves the conditions unclear.

## Preserve the research

Editorial repair does not authorise replacing experiments, proofs or methods.
Use the scope of the request to decide which scientific changes are permitted.
Keep unresolved alternatives in a research note.
Record why removed detail was redundant and where retained dependencies went.
