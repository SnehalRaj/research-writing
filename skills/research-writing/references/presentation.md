# Present the argument

## Figures and tables

Choose a figure for the scientific point it helps the reader understand.
Use a construction diagram to explain the method and a result plot to assess evidence.
Introduce the diagram's parts in the order the reader learns them.
A small operation can precede the full construction when the dependency requires it.

State the scientific point before the figure reference.
Keep the figure near its discussion, with readable labels at the intended page size.
Explain each panel, axis, unit and statistic in the caption.
Retain the conditions and uncertainty needed to interpret the result.
Keep editing history in working notes.

Preserve the source asset, data, scale and attribution.
Record and verify an authorised redraw against the source.
Use a consistent body font across tables.
Adjust columns and spacing before shrinking a table's whole contents.
Inspect the resulting page and its neighbours for gaps, clipping and interrupted explanations.

## Formal results

Use the manuscript's established definition, theorem and proof conventions.
Give reused statements stable labels and exact cross-references.
Define shared concepts once. Explain a new extension when the assumptions change.
Place a short proof near its statement when it helps the argument.
For an appendix proof, retain the main idea and consequence in the main text.
Check that the introductory account and formal statement describe the same object.

## Final output

Build the exact review revision.
Inspect the complete output after a complete rewrite.
Check figure order, table width, labels, captions and the start of each section.
Check bibliography links and internal reference destinations after edits.
Record the source and PDF identities in working notes.
Keep compilation, visual inspection and scientific verification as separate checks.
