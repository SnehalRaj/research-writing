# Critic: argument and prose

Reusable guide. This guide transfers an earlier writing method. Its review judgements have no independent accuracy benchmark.

## Task

Read the complete target section and its adjoining transitions.
Identify the research question, the author's position and each paragraph's job.
Report where the text loses that argument.

## Tests

1. Explain why each paragraph appears at this point.
2. Identify the relation between adjacent sentences.
3. Check whether the opening states a problem or point before its supporting facts.
4. Check whether results answer the motivating question.
5. Find repeated summaries, roadmaps and generic significance claims.
6. Find concrete reasons or evidence lost during compression.

Distinguish missing reasoning from missing transition words.
A useful finding explains the relation the reader lacks.
A request for smoother prose needs the missing relation and a repair action.

## Author style

Use the active voice chosen in the author brief and precise verbs.
Flag corrective juxtaposition, vague antecedents and stock future-work endings.
Keep scientific contrasts that state different conditions or mechanisms directly.
Treat quoted historical examples separately from new prose.
Preserve technical distinctions when removing rhetorical framing.

Do not flag a paragraph merely because it states a research choice rather than a measurable fact.
Check whether the choice is clear, relevant and supported by the following argument.
The author's interpretation is part of the research narrative.

## Required evidence

Quote the passage, state its intended job and identify the missing or repeated reasoning.
Name what must survive the proposed edit.
Use the common finding format. Mark taste judgements as optional.

## Failure to avoid

A synthetic boundary case in [B04](../cases/02-failures-and-boundaries.md#b04-a-shorter-opening-can-remove-the-research-reason) shortens an opening while removing the author's reasons.
This critic must flag that loss even if the new prose passes every word-level style check.

## Scope and preservation checks

Compare the original and revised argument before recommending a shorter version.
Preserve the research question, the author's reason and evidence that answers that question.
Keep a useful list when it helps readers compare independent items or follow steps.
A paragraph may contain supporting evidence and a necessary limit under its single main question.
Recheck the current opening when the conclusion answers an older question.
Treat approved navigation and emphatic repetition as protected structural choices.
