# Write for the reader

The style profile combines the author's Orwell and Zinsser rules with simplified technical English.
Use one idea per sentence. Aim for at most 25 words in descriptive sentences and 20 in instructions.
Treat those targets as prompts to read the text. Preserve a clear explanation when a longer sentence helps.
Use the active voice and familiar words. Retain established technical terms.
Repeat a term when synonyms would blur its meaning.
Keep necessary articles and causal links.

## Argument and flow

A paragraph has a purpose within the research argument.
Each sentence should explain, support, qualify or advance the preceding thought.
Use a connector when the evidence warrants the relationship.
Adding a connector cannot supply a missing reason.

Explain the difficulty before describing the chosen method.
Explain the construction before its registers, parameters or implementation details.
State the supported result before the configuration list.
Open a section with the context, problem or principle its reader needs.
Choose a heading that names the subject.
Use related work to explain the research choice and comparison.

Keep a correction's explanation in the passage that the reader will see.
Move the history of checking that explanation into notes.
Short sentences still need connected reasoning.
Read a revision aloud and check whether the original reasons survive.
A useful recap can support an independent reading of a section.
Remove repetition when two passages perform the same job without a reading need.

## AI writing tics

Avoid corrective juxtaposition, including the rhetorical forms "not X, but Y" and "X is not the problem; Y is".
State the actual claim directly.
Mathematical negation and a necessary scientific distinction retain their ordinary role.
Avoid stock significance, inflated novelty and claims that a framework "unlocks" an unexplained benefit.
Name the actor, action and object behind a vague phrase.
Use two examples when examples suffice.
Vary neighbouring sentence structures without adding ornamental vocabulary.
Avoid em dashes and generic announcements about what the next paragraph will say.

A word list can locate a suspect passage.
Read its meaning before changing it.
The word "robust" can describe a defined property or conceal an unmeasured claim.
Check which use the sentence makes.

## Limits and future work

Place a condition beside the claim that needs it.
Keep a concrete limit when it changes the result's interpretation.
Cut a repeated caution after the limit is already clear.
Make the conclusion answer the opening question.
State the contribution and evidence, then a useful next question.
Name the proposed experiment or derivation and explain what it would resolve.

## Compression

Cut empty significance claims, duplicated background and repeated navigation first.
Then relocate long proofs or settings that interrupt the main argument.
Preserve the mechanism, comparison, uncertainty and source contribution.
A longer passage can improve clarity when it supplies missing reasoning.
A shorter passage can fail when it removes that reasoning.

## Author voice

Use accepted passages and the author's account as examples of voice.
Preserve intentional terminology and protected wording.
Use "we" for a joint research contribution when the manuscript uses that voice.
Keep direct reviewer feedback distinct from a simulated reading perspective.
Avoid claiming that an absent reviewer approved a passage.
