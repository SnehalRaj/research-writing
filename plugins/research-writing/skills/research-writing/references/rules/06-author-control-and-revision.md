# Keep authorship and review decisions explicit

Use the author's current instructions to set the edit scope and review process.
A review record should show who decided what, which revision they saw and which checks support it.
The examples below are synthetic teaching examples.

## P01. Record the scope of each approval

Separate permission to plan, permission to draft and acceptance of finished wording.
Record the author, target passage, source revision and decision.
Keep new prose pending until the author accepts it.
When the request already authorises a review draft, complete that draft within its stated scope.

Use comments, a decision ledger or another explicit record to mark pending prose.
Colour can help readers identify changes, but colour alone leaves the approval state ambiguous.
A clean reading copy can serve a separate purpose from the version used to display edits.

Respect phase boundaries and the chosen review unit.
Approval of a scientific repair alone leaves later compression outside its scope unless the author also authorised that phase.
An explicit request can authorise a batch without fresh permission between units.
Record the batch scope and its final review state.

**Check:** Can another editor identify the exact wording and changes that the author accepted?

## P02. Preserve a recoverable baseline

Record the source revision and changed paths before a batch.
Compare the final diff with the agreed scope.
Check protected passages and result assets against the baseline.
Keep rejected drafts in history and restore the author's chosen baseline when requested.

**Synthetic example:** A shorter opening removes the reasons for a research choice that the author intended to preserve.
A style report still marks its short sentences as acceptable.
Restore the chosen baseline, recover the missing reasons and agree the next revision's scope.
The failure shows why sentence counts need an argument review.

**Check:** Can the author recover the chosen version and see why a proposed revision changed it?

## P03. Keep readiness states separate

Track source fidelity, scientific verification, editorial quality, rendered layout and author acceptance separately.
For external work, also distinguish preparation, upload, submission and institutional approval.
Use visible evidence for the state that the task requires.

A typeset chapter can still contain a disputed theorem.
A source claim can be copied accurately while its assumptions need qualification.
A completed upload alone leaves later institutional decisions open.
An accepted wording decision can require a new scientific check after the model changes.

**Check:** Does each reported state name its evidence and exact revision?
A build, checkpoint or critic report cannot stand in for a different review outcome.

## P04. Use critics as evidence-producing reviewers

Give each critic a bounded question, current revision and relevant sources.
Provide the contribution inventory, definition dependencies and protected author decisions.
Require exact locators, a reason, evidence and a proposed disposition.
A critic can recommend that a passage remain unchanged.

Keep direct reviewer comments separate from simulated style perspectives.
An actual annotation or supplied transcript can establish what that reviewer said within its context.
A reference document can inform structure and style.
It gives no evidence of what an absent author would personally decide.
Label a simulated perspective clearly and keep its status visible.

The author resolves taste and scope choices.
Mathematical claims need evidence even after the author accepts their wording.
Escalate a genuine scientific conflict clearly and continue independent work within scope.
Keep an unresolved finding open when available evidence cannot settle it.

**Check:** Can the editor trace each finding to its passage and distinguish evidence from a reviewer's interpretation?

## Resolve competing instructions by purpose

| Apparent conflict | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Explain more; shorten the document. | Add the missing mechanism, then cut repetition and unused detail. |
| Be bold; avoid overclaiming. | Lead with supported strengths and retain the exact comparison scope. |
| Remove roadmaps; add a section map. | Keep useful navigation once at each needed level. |
| Avoid technical detail; give full proofs. | Match detail to the section, audience and proof placement. |
| Preserve mathematical limits; remove disclaimers. | Keep conditions that change meaning and cut generic commentary around them. |

Use this table to interpret the purpose of a request.
A later explicit author decision controls the current task when it applies.
Record a genuine conflict and its evidence when the instructions cannot both hold.
