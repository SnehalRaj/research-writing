# Use the method for a research paper

Use this chapter with the [method](../method.md) and [claim checks](03-claims-proofs-evidence.md).
Treat paper adaptation as guidance whose usefulness needs evaluation on the actual task.
A transfer of thesis lessons alone establishes no validation for a separate paper-writing system.

Complete the source reading, contribution inventory and definition dependencies before choosing the paper's narrative.
Use the [inventory template](../templates/03-contributions-and-definitions.md) to retain every candidate and its evidence status.

## Start with a contribution, then choose the format

Write a short contribution brief from the verified material:

- The question and the specific gap.
- The answer and the evidence that supports it.

Choose the main claim after this exercise.
Check how the available results connect before presenting them as one argument.
Retain an unexplained or unresolved connection as an open issue in the plan.

For a methods paper, explain the design choice and show what it changes.
For a negative result, explain the belief or method tested and the consequence of its failure.
Both need an accurate comparison with prior work.
Choose the format around that contribution and the audience's needs.

## Compress the thesis structure

| Thesis practice | Paper adaptation |
|---|---|
| Chapter-to-chapter connection. | Connect directly to the field's problem and prior work. |
| Shared background chapters. | Give the minimal definitions needed for the contribution and cite standard details. |
| Several research questions. | Select one central argument and its supporting results. |
| Main-text proof idea with an appendix proof. | Keep this split when the venue permits supplementary material. |
| Chapter maps. | Use a short overview when the paper's complexity warrants it. |
| Separate author review of chapters. | Review coherent sections or claim groups. |

Keep necessary experimental details accessible through methods or supplementary material.
Check the venue's requirements before deciding their placement.
Map each omitted or moved contribution, figure and dependency to its destination or the reason for omission.
Compression should retain the construction, central evidence and essential conditions.

## Write the abstract after the argument is stable

Move from context to the obstacle, contribution and principal evidence.
End with what the combined result establishes or enables within its scope.
Use technical names when they help identify the contribution.

Choose the starting point for the audience.
A broad thesis abstract may need to introduce the field before reaching the technical challenge.
A specialist paper can begin closer to the research question.
Preserve the reasons that connect context, obstacle and result as the opening becomes more compact.

**Check:** Can a reader identify the question, contribution and main support without the rest of the paper?

## Calibrate the result sentence

Use a strong verb and a concrete outcome.
Retain the task, comparator and evidence type that make the sentence true.
Choose a metric or numerical result when it helps readers judge the contribution.
Check the actual table and selection procedure before choosing a leading-performance claim.

Describe a simulation result as a simulation result.
Keep a finite study's tested range beside the conclusion that depends on it.
Lead with a meaningful improvement when the evidence supports it.
Place precise conditions near the claim and cut generic cautions that repeat them.

**Check:** Does the sentence retain both the result's strength and the conditions that establish its scope?

## Preserve the author's contribution during review

Ask critics to report unsupported additions, missing evidence and lost contributions.
Require a reason before replacing a source figure or moving a central result to supplementary material.
Obtain author agreement when the task protects that asset or placement.
Keep the author's judgement about significance visible and distinguish it from a proven consequence.

After submission, preserve the reviewed version and its source records.
Treat reviewer requests as new findings with scope and evidence.
Let the author decide the response within the authorised revision process.
Track a revised draft, submitted revision and editorial decision as separate states.
