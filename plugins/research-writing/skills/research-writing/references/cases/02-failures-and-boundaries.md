# Failures and boundaries

Every scenario below is an original synthetic teaching example.
The scenarios describe possible decisions; they report no new experiment or measured critic performance.

<a id="b01"></a>

## B01. A source-preserving assembly can lose the argument

**Status:** original synthetic adaptation.

**Scenario:** An editor combines two source documents while retaining every quoted sentence. Each section now sends one necessary proof to the other section.

**Finding:** The merged document has a dependency cycle. It also lacks the reasons that connect its source contributions.

**Lesson:** Check the argument and dependency graph after assembly. Exact provenance cannot supply a missing antecedent or explanation.

**Preserve:** Keep the source map. Assign the proof one clear location and repair both references.

**Conditions:** Apply this check even when the source-sentence constraint passes. Avoid inventing a new result to fill the gap.

**Rules:** N02, N04, C03.

<a id="b02"></a>

## B02. An editorial task can exceed its scientific authority

**Status:** original synthetic adaptation.

**Scenario:** A user requests prose and layout edits. An audit finds a proof concern. The editor adds a stronger proposition and replaces a main plot with a nearby run.

**Finding:** The editor changed the contribution and experimental identity beyond the authorised scope.

**Lesson:** Record the evidence, affected claims and repair options. Obtain the author's decision before changing the research account.

**Preserve:** Retain the approved result asset and contribution inventory. Keep the scientific concern visibly open.

**Conditions:** Continue independent editorial work within scope. A local script or dataset does not authorise a substitute result.

**Rules:** P02, P04, C03, C04.

<a id="b03"></a>

## B03. Translate an annotation into a reader problem

**Status:** original synthetic adaptation.

**Scenario:** A reviewer finds a paragraph difficult to follow. The editor converts it into bullets, although the sentences need a causal explanation.

**Finding:** The format change leaves the missing reason unresolved.

**Lesson:** Identify the relation the reader lacks before choosing an edit. Add the necessary explanation and then choose the format.

**Preserve:** Keep a list when it serves independent choices or procedural steps.

**Conditions:** A connector helps only when the evidence supports that relation. Avoid treating one annotation as a global format rule.

**Rules:** N02.

<a id="b04"></a>

## B04. A shorter opening can remove the research reason

**Status:** original synthetic adaptation.

**Scenario:** The author explains that simulation makes controlled comparisons possible. A rewrite replaces this reason and its learning example with a broad efficiency claim.

**Finding:** The compressed opening loses an approved reason for the research choice.

**Lesson:** Compare the argument before and after a large cut. Restore the reason, remove unrelated facts and repair local transitions.

**Preserve:** Keep the approved learning example and research position. Preserve a recoverable baseline and unrelated approved edits.

**Conditions:** If the author rejects the rewrite, restore the named scope and compare it with the baseline. Sentence length does not establish argument quality.

**Rules:** N03, L03, P02.

<a id="b05"></a>

## B05. A scientific qualification can resemble an empty caveat

**Status:** original synthetic adaptation.

**Scenario:** A style pass removes the statement that a magnitude score ignores coefficient signs. The displayed result uses only that score.

**Finding:** The cut broadens the interpretation of the measurement.

**Lesson:** Assess the information in a qualification before judging its tone. Retain conditions and invariances that change the result's meaning.

**Preserve:** Keep the metric's exact limitation and the measured comparison.

**Conditions:** Remove only commentary that adds no local condition, example or consequence. Check captions and summaries for the same scope.

**Rules:** X01, X05, C04.

## B06. A broad family name can hide a narrow representation claim

**Status:** original synthetic adaptation.

**Scenario:** An editor replaces several gate-family names with one excitation-conserving label. A later representation formula assumes a particular connectivity and fixed mode order.

**Finding:** The new label hides conditions required by the mathematical representation.

**Lesson:** Map each term to its object, domain, equivalence conditions and exceptions before replacing it. Check ordinary and controlled gates separately.

**Preserve:** Keep the gate family, connectivity, mode order and subspace assumptions. Distinguish a component layer from the complete model.

**Conditions:** A shared conservation rule alone does not prove a family equivalence. A search can locate occurrences; each occurrence still needs a meaning check.

**Rules:** E05, C02.

<a id="b07"></a>

## B07. A strong result needs its bounded comparison

**Status:** original synthetic adaptation.

**Scenario:** A method improves the measured accuracy with fewer trained parameters on two tasks. A rewrite calls it superior to all existing methods.

**Finding:** The ranking exceeds the tasks, baselines and budgets tested.

**Lesson:** State the strongest supported improvement clearly. Keep the task, comparator, metric and parameter budget beside it.

**Preserve:** Retain the measured strength and the quality-cost trade-off that makes it useful.

**Conditions:** Parameter count alone does not establish useful task performance. A hardware agreement experiment supplies a different comparison from predictive accuracy.

**Rules:** N01, N05, C04.

<a id="b08"></a>

## B08. A deliberate structural choice can survive reviewer advice

**Status:** original synthetic adaptation.

**Scenario:** A reviewer proposes removing repeated research questions. The author has approved their return in the conclusion before each answer.

**Finding:** The repetition has a recorded reading purpose and explicit author acceptance.

**Lesson:** A critic supplies the finding and reason. The author decides an accurate structural or taste choice. Record the decision for later reviews.

**Preserve:** Keep the approved question wording, current source and acceptance scope.

**Conditions:** Reopen the unit when evidence changes or the author requests it. Unapproved duplicate roadmaps can still need removal.

**Rules:** N06, P01, X04.
