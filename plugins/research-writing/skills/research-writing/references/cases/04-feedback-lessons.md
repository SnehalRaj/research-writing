# Feedback lessons with their boundaries

This guide transfers 66 coded lessons from a private feedback record. The wording below gives reusable guidance.
It contains no private reviewer quotation or manuscript passage. The private audit keeps the exact source and attribution.
The record used purposive coding. Its counts do not estimate how often a problem occurs.
Dates of source archiving and first recorded quotations do not establish the date of an undated spoken comment.

Apply each lesson through the current author brief. Keep the boundary with the lesson.

## F01. Narrative

Explain the relation between consecutive sentences. Supply the reason that lets the reader follow that relation.

**Boundary and reason:** A connector helps when the reasoning already supports it. Check the repaired argument after adding the connector.

**Related rules:** N02.

## F02. Narrative

Read the paragraph again after a transition edit. Confirm that the new wording explains why the next sentence follows.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep lists that help readers compare independent items. Choose a paragraph or list from its reader task.

**Related rules:** N02.

## F03. Narrative

Give a paragraph one main question. Develop its answer with the evidence that serves that question.

**Boundary and reason:** Supporting evidence and a necessary qualification can share the paragraph. Avoid a rigid one-sentence-per-paragraph rule.

**Related rules:** N02.

## F04. Narrative

Ask the author to explain a difficult argument aloud. Record the problem, intended claim and reason before editing.

**Boundary and reason:** The explanation records the author's intent. Check its scientific content against the source and assumptions.

**Related rules:** N03.

## F05. Narrative

Explain the obstacle that each method choice addresses. Connect that choice to the capability the section needs.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep the explanation local. Avoid a fresh broad introduction before every formula.

**Related rules:** N01, E01.

## F06. Narrative

Introduce the construction and its purpose before its notation. Order the details by the reader's dependencies.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep the mathematical conditions at the point where the construction uses them.

**Related rules:** E01.

## F07. Narrative

Explain parameter efficiency through useful task quality and its training cost. State the quality-cost question before describing frozen parameters.

**Boundary and reason:** A lower parameter count supplies only one part of that comparison. Retain the accuracy evidence needed for usefulness.

**Related rules:** N01, N05.

## F08. Narrative

State the author's research position in direct prose. Give the reasons that the following evidence supports.

**Boundary and reason:** An interpretation retains the premises of every theorem it uses. Keep scientific intent separate from an overstated formulation.

**Related rules:** N03, L01.

## F09. Narrative

Open a historical account with a verified result that explains its research role. Identify the actual competition or comparison behind it.

**Boundary and reason:** A competition ranking supports that bounded comparison. A broader comparison with human performance needs its own evidence.

**Related rules:** N05, C03.

## F10. Narrative

Remove a true fact when the argument gives it no role. Use the paragraph map to check that judgement.

**Boundary and reason:** A commercial statistic can serve a study of adoption. Its relevance depends on the current research question.

**Related rules:** N02, X01.

## F11. Narrative

Preserve the specific reasons that the author approved when compressing an opening. Compare the research argument before and after the cut.

**Boundary and reason:** Shorter sentences and more transitions can still remove the reason for a research choice.

**Related rules:** N03, P02.

## F12. Narrative

Make the conclusion answer the questions in the current introduction. Check the live source before reusing a chapter map.

**Boundary and reason:** An older map may contain superseded questions. Update the affected connection and record the source version.

**Related rules:** N06.

## F13. Structure

Answer each research question with its result and implication. Restate the question when the author chose that reading structure.

**Boundary and reason:** Repetition can support navigation. Retain its approved wording and scope rather than applying a blanket deletion rule.

**Related rules:** N06.

## F14. Structure

Keep one useful guide to the document structure at each needed level. Remove duplicate guides that perform the same job.

**Boundary and reason:** A contribution chapter can still need a local guide. Judge its audience and location separately.

**Related rules:** X04, N06.

## F15. Structure

Place a requested chapter map after the motivation and contribution when that order serves the author's brief.

**Boundary and reason:** This local choice does not require a new roadmap in every section. Keep the map accurate after restructuring.

**Related rules:** N06.

## F16. Structure

Assign detail from the section's purpose. A technical appendix can carry a full derivation needed to check a result.

**Boundary and reason:** Include relevant detail. Remove background that neither the proof nor the experimental specification uses.

**Related rules:** X03.

## F17. Structure

Begin an appendix at its first substantive unit when a prefatory summary adds no job. Follow the author's chosen structure.

**Boundary and reason:** Retain definitions and assumptions required by the first proof. An opening cut must preserve those dependencies.

**Related rules:** X04.

## F18. Structure

Give the main proof idea and scientific consequence beside a theorem whose detailed proof sits in an appendix.

**Boundary and reason:** The sketch keeps the key assumption. Preserve the full proof and a precise cross-reference.

**Related rules:** V03.

## F19. Structure

Place a short proof directly after its proposition when it fits the argument. Use the established proof environment and ending marker.

**Boundary and reason:** A long supporting derivation may belong in an appendix. Keep its main idea accessible in the main text.

**Related rules:** V03.

## F20. Structure

Check every later dependency before cutting technical preliminaries. Record which result uses each retained equation.

**Boundary and reason:** Preserve equations that a proof or experiment needs. Give removed material a destination when it remains necessary.

**Related rules:** X03.

## F21. Structure

Recover the source paper's question and explanatory order before restructuring methods. Read its introduction and abstract with the source inventory.

**Boundary and reason:** The chapter still needs its own relation to the thesis question. Source wording alone cannot supply that connection.

**Related rules:** N04, C03.

## F22. Pedagogy

Define each mathematical object and its space before an equation uses it. Keep the symbol and the object consistent.

**Boundary and reason:** Use the shortest definition that supports later work. A complete textbook survey rarely serves the same job.

**Related rules:** E02.

## F23. Pedagogy

Build quantum preliminaries through their dependencies. Introduce states before operations and measurements when the explanation uses that order.

**Boundary and reason:** Explain how a quantum state differs from a classical probability distribution. Avoid silently treating their representations as interchangeable.

**Related rules:** E01, E02.

## F24. Pedagogy

Use circuit diagrams to connect the mathematical operation to its preparation, transformation and measurement steps. Explain the circuit notation.

**Boundary and reason:** A workflow diagram can show the broad process while leaving wires and gates unexplained. Check the audience's actual gap.

**Related rules:** E04, V01.

## F25. Pedagogy

Give comparable estimator equations before a shared analysis. State the sampling rule and the target quantity for each estimator.

**Boundary and reason:** Shared notation must preserve differences in bias and variance. Keep each estimator's assumptions accessible.

**Related rules:** E03.

## F26. Pedagogy

Compare resource costs with a common notation and cost definition. Give the precision target and unit for every estimate.

**Boundary and reason:** Check evaluation counts, measurement shots and elapsed time separately. Converting between them needs explicit assumptions.

**Related rules:** E03, C04.

## F27. Pedagogy

Expand an acronym at the intended audience's first encounter. Identify the method even when the first use occurs in an abstract or summary.

**Boundary and reason:** Verify the actual occurrence in the current source. A report about an older location may need correction.

**Related rules:** E02.

## F28. Pedagogy

Name the physical register as the reader encounters it. Then explain the mathematical modes and their encoding relation.

**Boundary and reason:** Qubits and modes can describe different objects. State the encoding before using one term to replace the other.

**Related rules:** E05.

## F29. Pedagogy

Map related terms to their definitions, domains and equivalence conditions. Check each global replacement against that map.

**Boundary and reason:** A shared conservation rule cannot identify the whole gate family. Retain connectivity, order and representation conditions when they matter.

**Related rules:** E05, C02.

## F30. Pedagogy

Replace an unclear antecedent with the actual object or source. Link it to a definition the reader can locate.

**Boundary and reason:** Verify that the definition exists. If a move removed it, restore the definition or give a valid reference.

**Related rules:** E02, E05.

## F31. Claims

Keep feature-parameter reuse within the component that the result covers. Treat finite gradient observations as their own evidence.

**Boundary and reason:** A fixed feature architecture supplies no full-model trainability proof. Preserve the parameter and quantifier boundaries.

**Related rules:** C01, C02.

## F32. Claims

After narrowing a claim, search the abstract, summaries and captions for the same claim. Repair every affected occurrence.

**Boundary and reason:** A caveat beside one occurrence leaves another overstatement intact. Compare each sentence with its own evidence.

**Related rules:** C01.

## F33. Claims

Retain a derivation when its definitions and inferences can be defended. Make the justification for each step explicit.

**Boundary and reason:** A precise cited result can replace an unused derivation. Verify its identity, assumptions and role before the cut.

**Related rules:** C05, X03.

## F34. Claims

Verify that each citation names the right work and supports the attached claim. Check its exact theorem or result locator.

**Boundary and reason:** A working link establishes access. It leaves the source's assumptions and the claim's support to be checked.

**Related rules:** C03.

## F35. Claims

Treat an ordering rule as part of the equivariance construction. Specify how the input metadata and schedule transform under relabelling.

**Boundary and reason:** Canonical labels, transported order and choices fixed under automorphisms require separate arguments. Keep the actual convention with the theorem.

**Related rules:** C02, C06.

## F36. Claims

Carry a theorem's domain and input convention into early contribution claims. Recheck summaries when the formal result changes.

**Boundary and reason:** An augmented input with transported order has its own scope. Preserve that scope wherever the result appears.

**Related rules:** C02.

## F37. Claims

Compare state preparation with the exact input assumed by the theorem. Check the weights and normalisation used in both.

**Boundary and reason:** Handle zero feature rows and later preparation reuse explicitly. A uniform-input theorem may leave a weighted preparation outside its assumptions.

**Related rules:** C02, C04.

## F38. Claims

Describe the predictor computed by the experiment. Trace the combination of vectors or states through the actual output function.

**Boundary and reason:** An amplitude-vector ensemble and a density-state predictor have different readout meanings. Preserve numerical results while a description alone is disputed.

**Related rules:** C04.

## F39. Claims

Identify the object a baseline changes before comparing methods. Check whether the intervention acts on weights or hidden representations.

**Boundary and reason:** A shared adaptation goal does not make these interventions equivalent. Keep the operation and comparator settings precise.

**Related rules:** E05, C04.

## F40. Claims

State a supported improvement clearly and early. Retain the task, metric and parameter budget in the result sentence.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep the tested comparator set. A task-specific result cannot support an unrestricted superiority claim.

**Related rules:** N05, C04.

## F41. Claims

Report predictive performance and hardware agreement as separate outcomes. Give the evidence and tested tolerance for the hardware comparison.

**Boundary and reason:** Agreement with a simulation within tolerance cannot establish a hardware accuracy advantage. Retain the actual model-quality comparison.

**Related rules:** N05, C04.

## F42. Editing

Record each broad wording edit with its before and after text. Include the source revision and exact location.

**Boundary and reason:** Log deletions explicitly. Keep the scientific conditions and the destination of moved detail in the same review record.

**Related rules:** P01.

## F43. Editing

Name the unresolved action and its scientific object when future work helps the argument. Place it beside the open construction or in the discussion.

**Boundary and reason:** A repeated generic closing sentence adds no task. Use a concrete proposal only where it serves the section.

**Related rules:** X02.

## F44. Editing

Remove a generic requirement when it has no local example, action or consequence. Check its role before deleting it.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep conditions that define a theorem, measured result or experimental interpretation. Their scientific role controls the decision.

**Related rules:** X01.

## F45. Editing

Use results prose to explain the finding and its meaning. Move settings that obscure that finding to the appropriate methods section.

**Boundary and reason:** Retain necessary implementation detail in an accessible location. Record that destination so compression preserves reproducibility.

**Related rules:** X03, N05.

## F46. Editing

Use the author's agreed term consistently within its scientific meaning. Record the preference in the terminology map.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep exact publication titles and source quotations. A preferred label cannot erase a mathematical distinction.

**Related rules:** E05, L02.

## F47. Editing

Keep an analogy when it explains a mechanism or choice in the contribution. Remove it when it serves no role in the current argument.

**Boundary and reason:** Judge the local teaching value. A rejected analogy supplies no blanket rule against useful comparisons elsewhere.

**Related rules:** X01.

## F48. Editing

Keep a caption focused on the figure and the information needed to interpret it. Move unrelated editorial explanation into the working record.

**Boundary and reason:** Place a scientific limit where readers need it. A shorter caption must preserve an accurate account of the plotted result.

**Related rules:** V02, X05.

## F49. Editing

Use an active verb that names the author's scientific action. Match that verb to the claim's evidence type.

**Boundary and reason:** A proof and a measurement establish different results. Select the verb only after classifying the claim.

**Related rules:** L01.

## F50. Editing

State the actual relation between neighbouring approaches. Explain their shared purpose or distinct operations when the argument needs that relation.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep mathematically meaningful differences explicit. A style change must preserve the objects and conditions that differ.

**Related rules:** L02, N02.

## F51. Figures

Compare the built page with the requested figure placement. Inspect the page at the intended reading size and record its identity.

**Boundary and reason:** A float directive records an instruction. The rendered page supplies the placement evidence. Refresh page locators after the build.

**Related rules:** V01.

## F52. Figures

Split a composite display when each plot supports a different claim and the split improves its placement. Check the surrounding pages.

**Boundary and reason:** Preserve the original data and readable labels. Keep the relationship between plots clear after changing the layout.

**Related rules:** V01.

## F53. Figures

Use panels to explain related stages of a mechanism. Give each panel a clear purpose and a link to the text.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep the original scientific content and attribution. Make the caption explain how the stages relate.

**Related rules:** E04.

## F54. Figures

Explain the scientific point before the figure reference. Use the figure as evidence or explanation for that point.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep visual detail in the caption unless the argument uses it. Avoid repeating every panel feature in the prose.

**Related rules:** V01.

## F55. Figures

Remove implementation detail from results prose or captions when it obscures the result. Retain a clear route to the methods.

**Boundary and reason:** Keep axes, statistics and interpretation conditions with the figure. Retain uncertainty and experimental scope when they affect its meaning.

**Related rules:** V02, X03.

## F56. Workflow

Respect an agreed phase sequence for scientific repair, explanation and compression. Record the author decision at each required boundary.

**Boundary and reason:** Process approval covers that process. Later wording and unrelated scientific changes retain their own acceptance requirements.

**Related rules:** P01.

## F57. Workflow

Mark new prose and edits as pending review using the agreed mechanism. Keep their status in the working record.

**Boundary and reason:** Text colour can serve a reading format. Infer acceptance from the explicit decision and its scope.

**Related rules:** P01.

## F58. Workflow

Give the author both printed and PDF page locators for a review batch. Tie those locators to the inspected file identity.

**Boundary and reason:** Update the locators after the final build. Source line numbers and old page positions cannot substitute for the current rendered file.

**Related rules:** P01, V01.

## F59. Workflow

Record the author's exact acceptance response and the unit it covers. Link that decision to the reviewed revision.

**Boundary and reason:** Approval of a chapter leaves an unreviewed appendix open. Preserve all scope exclusions in the acceptance record.

**Related rules:** P01.

## F60. Workflow

When the author rejects a rewrite, restore the agreed baseline for the affected scope. Compare the restored files with that baseline.

**Boundary and reason:** Preserve unrelated approved changes. Record exact restoration boundaries and source comparisons rather than applying a broad reset.

**Related rules:** P02.

## F61. Workflow

Retain deliberate repetition when the author accepts its structural purpose. Record a declined reviewer suggestion and the reason.

**Boundary and reason:** Later critics should use that decision as context. Reopen the unit when new evidence or an author request requires review.

**Related rules:** P01, X04.

## F62. Workflow

Read the rendered document for editorial residue and placeholder text. Check included captions and tables as well as the main source.

**Boundary and reason:** A clean rendered file establishes one review outcome. Scientific verification and author acceptance still need their own evidence.

**Related rules:** P03.

## F63. Abstracts

For a lay abstract, lead from field motivation to the obstacle, contribution and main result. Match terms to the intended audience.

**Boundary and reason:** Accessible language still needs precise result claims. Retain the evidence type and the scope that make each result true.

**Related rules:** X03, N01.

## F64. Abstracts

Name what the framework contributes. Give enough mechanism or capability for the audience to distinguish it from a broad description of its data.

**Boundary and reason:** Preserve the limits of expressivity, training and benchmark evidence. A short abstract cannot silently broaden any of them.

**Related rules:** N05, E02.

## F65. Abstracts

End the abstract with the combined contribution when the author chooses that structure. State what the evidence establishes within its scope.

**Boundary and reason:** A discussion can name a concrete future study. The abstract-ending choice does not prohibit future work throughout the document.

**Related rules:** N06, X02.

## F66. Abstracts

Review a translation against the argument and paragraph jobs of the accepted source version. Compare every claim's assumptions and scope.

**Boundary and reason:** Acceptance of the source language does not accept the translation. Record the translated revision and its own decision.

**Related rules:** C01, P01.
