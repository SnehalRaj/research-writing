# Critic: exposition and terminology

Reusable guide. This guide transfers an earlier writing method. Its review judgements have no independent accuracy benchmark.

## Task

Read as the intended audience, using only definitions already introduced or clearly referenced.
Check whether the reader can reconstruct the method and understand why each part is present.

## Tests

1. Identify every new term, symbol and acronym at first use.
2. Check its object, domain and meaning against the notation record.
3. Check whether the construction precedes its implementation details.
4. Check the explanation before and after each important equation.
5. Check the conditions under which two terms or constructions are equivalent.
6. Check background dependencies before recommending a cut.

Use a short trace for a mechanism: input, operation, output and role in the model.
If the trace cannot be completed, identify the missing definition or step.
Recommend a diagram or small example only when it resolves that gap.

## Section scope

Keep introductions focused on the problem, choice and high-level results.
Keep methods precise enough to understand the construction.
Allow proofs and appendices the technical detail they need.
A general preference for short prose cannot decide all three cases.

## Failure to avoid

Do not unify names because they look similar.
Distinguish a conservation law from a gate family, a representation from a state, and a module from the complete model.
Give each proposed terminology change an explicit scope and a source.

## Dependency and comparison checks

For quantum preliminaries, distinguish a probability distribution from a quantum state.
Introduce states before operations and measurements when those dependencies apply.
Relate physical registers to mathematical modes through the actual encoding.
A register name alone cannot establish the representation.

Expand acronyms at each independent entry point used by the audience.
Check abstracts, captions and chapter summaries as well as the main text.
Give competing methods comparable equations and name the resource unit in each comparison.
Preserve differences in assumptions and estimator behaviour.

A circuit diagram must explain its notation and operations.
A diagram of workflow boxes may leave that need open.
Use panels when they explain related stages; give each panel a role in the argument.
Retain established technical terms and exact publication titles.
Check the author's term preference against the current scientific meaning.
